=== 10. INTERPRETATION BOUNDARY ===
  Model trained on clean synthetic J-V only. If sigma is insensitive or calibration
  degrades under input noise, the correct reading is that the Gaussian head
  characterizes conditional predictive uncertainty within the CLEAN simulation
  domain, and does not explicitly model measurement-noise uncertainty.
  Preprocessing note (corrected): the reverse-tail-dominated MinMax compresses
  the ENTIRE forward-bias J-V (raw ~0..|Jsc|) into the top ~0.4% of the
  normalized input ([0.9964, 1.0]). A physical 1% noise (sigma=0.01*|Jsc|)
  therefore maps to ~1% of the forward-region signal amplitude and is nearly
  invisible in the reverse tail. So the perturbation acts ~1:1 on the
  physically informative region - the observed degradation is a genuine model
  response, not a preprocessing attenuation artifact.

=== 11. SCIENTIFIC CONCLUSIONS (data-only) ===

A. POINT PREDICTION
   -> MODERATELY ROBUST at <=1% of Jsc.
      At 1% noise: mean Pearson 0.749 (clean 0.807, -7%); 7/9 params keep r>0.82;
      the two intrinsically-hard params (tau_ETL, rad_c) fall to ~0.51 (from
      0.56-0.59). At 2% (stress test): mean r=0.672, worst r=0.41-0.44, and
      relative MAE increases 5-40%. The recoverability hierarchy is INVARIANT
      under noise (bulk transport/lifetime > interfaces >= radiative at every
      level) - a strong structural result.

B. PREDICTED SIGMA
   -> NO CLEAR RESPONSE.
      sigma is essentially flat vs input noise (mu_n 0.0927->0.0918, tau_ETL
      0.2295->0.2303, rad_c 0.2283->0.2243; max change <0.006, MC SD ~1e-4).
      The Gaussian head does NOT increase sigma with measurement noise. It
      characterizes predictive uncertainty within the clean simulation domain
      only; it never learned an explicit measurement-noise response.

C. CALIBRATION
   -> DEGRADES (coverage collapses, widths do not widen).
      95% coverage drops 0.946-0.969 (clean) -> 0.836-0.934 (1%) -> 0.723-0.887
      (2%), while mean interval width stays ~constant (sigma flat). This is the
      signature of out-of-training-distribution input: the model is a calibrated
      uncertainty model for clean in-domain J-V, NOT a measurement-noise model.
      Strong params lose the most coverage (Nc 0.946->0.836 at 1%) because their
      sigma is tight; wide-sigma params (tau_ETL, rad_c) lose least absolutely.

D. PARAMETER HIERARCHY
   Most robust (Pearson degradation R at 1%): tau_n (+0.030), tau_p (+0.030),
   mu_p (+0.070), mu_n (+0.072).
   Least robust: tau_ETL (+0.132), Nc (+0.096), Nv (+0.084), rad_c (+0.081).
   By RELATIVE MAE increase, the tightly-bounded strong params degrade most:
   Nc (+41%), mu_p (+30%), mu_n (+27%), Nv (+29%); rad_c least (+5%).
   Bulk lifetimes are the most noise-robust; DOS and tau_ETL the most
   noise-sensitive; absolute-accuracy hierarchy preserved at all levels.

E. MANUSCRIPT IMPLICATION
   Cannot claim 'robust to realistic J-V measurement noise' in the sense that
   the model's uncertainty accounts for measurement noise - sigma does not
   adapt and calibration collapses. Defensible claim: 'retains useful inversion
   accuracy for the well-recoverable parameters under moderate synthetic current
   perturbations (<=1% of Jsc), with an unchanged recoverability hierarchy; the
   predictive sigma does not adapt to input noise, so calibration degrades under
   measurement perturbations.' Any experimental-use sentence must carry this
   boundary, or be supported by retraining with measurement-noise augmentation
   (future work).

F. ADDITIONAL FINDINGS
   - Voltage offsets <= +-5 mV are negligible (r_mean 0.805-0.807 ~= clean 0.807).
   - Current scale error matters more, and asymmetrically: +-0.2% -> r_mean
     0.801-0.803, +-0.5% -> 0.775-0.788, +-1% -> 0.725-0.753 (negative bias
     slightly worse than positive).
   - 1% noise corresponds to physical J-V RMSE 0.21 mA/cm2, MAE 0.17 mA/cm2,
     max|d| 0.60 mA/cm2 (median |Jsc| ~ 23.7 mA/cm2).
   - HV (V>0.8 V) region carries the same perturbation magnitude as the full
     curve; exploratory Pearson(hv-perturb, |err| increase) is weak (0.06-0.12)
     - no evidence the HV segment dominates parameter-error growth (observational).

G. KEY INTERPRETATION BOUNDARY
   The current Gaussian head characterizes conditional predictive uncertainty
   within the clean simulation domain. The coverage collapse under input noise is
   the expected consequence of an OOD input, NOT a model failure. To make the
   model's sigma respond to measurement noise, training must include noisy J-V
   augmentation (out of scope here; no retraining was performed).
